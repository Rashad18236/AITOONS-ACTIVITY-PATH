# Flight Path Poster

Local web tool that turns a FlightRadar24 track export (KML/CSV) into three 3D-printable files:
`frame.3mf` (black backplate + ring, white text as its own body), `relief.3mf` (multi-body
green terrain / blue water / grey high-alpine band, one AMS slot each) and `path.stl` (the flight-path strip:
a red wall following the flight altitude, standing in a constant-floor slot).

## Rules

- Printed in PLA on a Bambu, 256×256mm plate, poster is one piece (default 250mm)
- Design decisions with Fable, implementation via Opus subagents
- Ponytail full mode during coding: laziest working solution, heightmap ops over 3D booleans, no speculative features
- Local only (localhost), single user, no auth, no persistence

## Run

```bash
uv run uvicorn app:app --reload
# open http://localhost:8000
```

## Architecture

- `app.py` — FastAPI: upload/generate routes, serves `static/`
- `poster.py` — core: parse track → fetch DEM (AWS Terrain Tiles, terrarium, no key) + water polygons (OSM via Overpass) → compose 2D heightmaps (frame + text mask, green/water/snow relief, slot) → mesh to `frame.3mf` + `relief.3mf` + `path.stl`
- `static/index.html` — upload form, parameter sliders, three.js preview, downloads
- `test_poster.py` — self-check: parse sample track, build small mesh, assert watertight + strip fits slot

Key invariant: every body comes off the same heightmap, so they always fit. The relief plate glues into the frame ring (fit clearance ceil-rounded up to a whole pixel, ≥0.2mm per side — rounding down gives an interference fit); the strip glues into the slot with 0.30mm per side. The slot is carved to a constant `SLOT_FLOOR_MM = 1.04` floor (depth varies with terrain, the floor does not), and the strip runs from that floor up to the flight altitude on the terrain's own vertical scale, clamped to at least `proud_mm` above the terrain under each station.

The DEM is blurred once (`smooth_px` = 1px) and the terrain surface is snapped to a printable layer boundary before caps and slot are cut, so flat ground slices as uniform layers. The grid is `FIRST_LAYER_MM + n*layer_mm` (0.2 + n*0.12), *not* `n*layer_mm` — the printer's first layer is thicker than the rest. One helper, `_snap(z, p)`, does it for the terrain, the strip top and the `z_snow` plane, and every fixed thickness is a whole layer count (`SLOT_FLOOR_MM` 1.04, water `cap_mm` 0.96, text 1.20, `base_mm` 4.04, `frame_height_mm` 9.96 at 250mm, `proud_mm` 0.96 at 250mm). The *scaled* thicknesses are not whole layer counts by luck, so `resolve()` snaps them: `_layers(v, p)` is `_snap`'s delta form and rounds `frame_height_mm`, `proud_mm` and `relief_mm` (the last through `exaggeration`, which is what reaches the relief ceiling) **up** to whole layers — up, because each has a printability floor that rounding down can cross. Without it a 170mm poster put the ring top at 10.81mm, 0.05mm above layer boundary 88, and the slicer's rounding — not the geometry — decided which layer the black/white colour boundary landed on. `frame_mm`, `text_mm`, `groove_w_mm` and `chin_mm` are in-plane and are deliberately never snapped.

**Grey is an elevation band, not a cap.** The terrain solid is split at the horizontal plane `z_snow` (= `snow_line_m` on the relief's own mm scale, snapped): green below, a grey solid from that flat plane up to the surface above. A conformal 0.6mm grey cap surfaced as a 0.26mm stripe on median alpine slope — under one extrusion width, which the slicer merges into the neighbouring colour ("blotted grey"). The two bodies meet exactly at `z_snow` and their union is still the same surface heightmap, so this changes colour volume, not shape. The footprint is the bare `elev >= snow_line_m` threshold intersected with `surface > z_snow` — no morphology: an opening dropped small peaks from grey while leaving them in green, so green towered above the plane and the two colours shared layers. A small peak is a solid band volume that prints fine, and cell-based meshing already drops sub-2-cell blobs. Water stays a conformal `cap_mm` cap — lakes are flat.

**The caption bar is a chin, and the text has its own grid.** `size_mm` is the poster's **width** and the square relief area; `chin_mm` is extra frame hung *below* it, so the frame grid is `(N + chin_px, N)` while `N`, `M` and `fpx` keep their formulas — the ring opening and the relief are unchanged by construction (byte-identical, and pinned by a test). A poster therefore measures `size_mm` × `size_mm + chin_mm`, and **both** must fit the 256 plate; `check_output.py`'s footprint rule is per axis for that reason. `chin_mm` is auto: it is whatever `text`/`text2` need beyond the ring's own bottom bar, `block + 2*TEXT_MARGIN_MM - gridded_ring` rounded up to a pixel, so no caption means no chin and every earlier poster is unchanged. `gridded_ring` is `(int(frame_mm/res_mm) - 1) * res_mm` — the bar *as the mesher lays it out*, since frame rows `N-fpx..H-1` span one pixel less than the nominal `frame_mm + chin_mm`. Line 2 is a subtitle at `SUB_RATIO` (0.65) of line 1 with `GAP_RATIO` (0.35) between them. Note the auto chin fires for **any** caption: a single-line poster at 250mm defaults is now 2.4mm taller than before this change (252.4 vs 250), and a two-line one is 10.4mm taller — which puts 250mm two-line **over** the 256 plate. ~245mm is the practical two-line ceiling; `check_output.py`'s y rule is what says so.

The text body is meshed on its **own** grid, `TEXT_SUB` (4) times finer than the frame's — 0.1mm at defaults. It sits on the flat ring, so it needs only a constant top array, not the frame heightmap, and that is what decouples the two grids. The mask is `(H-1)*TEXT_SUB+1 × (N-1)*TEXT_SUB+1`, **not** `H*TEXT_SUB × N*TEXT_SUB`: `heightmap_mesh` and `_slab_mesh` both map row → `(rows-1-row)*res`, and that `-1` is a vertex count that does not scale with `TEXT_SUB`. Sized naively the caption sat `(1 - 1/TEXT_SUB)*res_mm` = 0.3mm north of the frame on every row. Sized this way fine row `r*TEXT_SUB` **is** frame row `r`, exactly, in both axes and at every resolution — pinned by `test_the_text_grid_is_registered_to_the_frame_grid`, the only test that puts the two meshes side by side. On the terrain's 0.4mm grid an 8mm capital was ~20px tall with 0.4mm stair-steps and a 0.4mm dilation fattening every stroke; at 4× the edge pitch and the dilation both cost 0.1mm (250mm "HB-EZE": 2796 → 27304 verts, ink area 175.7 → 124.5mm²). 6× buys 0.6% more fidelity for 2.2× the vertices, so 4× it is.

**The caption is set bold and its strokes are floored.** A 0.4mm nozzle cannot hold a stroke under ~0.8mm (1.0mm recommended): at 1.5–2.2 extrusion widths a glyph is pure perimeter and Arachne flips between one and two beads along a single stem. `FONT_PATHS` therefore tries the box's **bold** faces first (`(path, face index)` pairs — Helvetica.ttc is a collection whose index 1 is Bold), and `_text_mask` then renders each caption line on its own and calls `_dilation_px`/`_dilate`: `_stroke_mm` measures the rendered stroke by successive erosion, and the line is dilated until it clears `MIN_STROKE_MM` (1.0mm), on top of the 1 fine pixel the mesher's four-corner cell rule needs anyway. Per line, because line 2 is the smaller face and needs the most. The erosion stops at three quarters of the ink gone, not at an empty mask — eroding to empty measures the *fattest* feature on the line, which for "ZURICH → ALTENRHEIN" is the arrowhead (1.60mm against a 1.1mm stem) and would leave every stem unthickened. Total dilation is capped at a quarter of the cap height so it cannot weld a counter shut, and says so on stdout if that cap engages. **And the dilation is paid for in tracking.** Growing a glyph by `n` fine pixels on every side eats `n` off *each* side of every gap, so a line dilated by `n` and left at its designed advances closes any gap under `2n` — at `size_mm=170` the subtitle took `n=2` and `"HB-EZE · 02.08.2026"` came out as 7 blobs where it has 17 letters, which is the opposite of the readability the thickening exists for. `ImageDraw.text` has no tracking parameter, so `_line_mask` lays the line out itself: pen x accumulates `font.getlength(ch) + 2n` and each glyph is drawn on its own, giving back exactly what the dilation took. `n` is measured off a first, untracked render (it depends only on the rendered stroke); the tracking then widens the line, so the width fit is redone against the **tracked** ink or a long caption walks off the bar, and `n` is deliberately *not* re-measured after that shrink — tracking and dilation have to stay the same number, and a hair under `MIN_STROKE_MM` beats letters that touch. The price is kerning: per-character advances only, so a "VA" pair sits a shade loose, which is the direction this is pushing anyway. Pinned by `test_the_stroke_floor_does_not_merge_the_caption_letters`, which counts 4-connected ink blobs per line and requires at least one per ink-bearing character (`>=`, not `==`: an accented capital is legitimately two blobs).

Visual params (`frame_mm`, `relief_mm`, `frame_height_mm`, `text_mm`, `groove_w_mm`, `proud_mm`) default to `None` = auto: `poster.resolve(p)` scales them from their 250mm base by `size_mm` and floors them for printability, so an 80mm poster keeps the 250mm look. `build_poster` and `check_output.py` both resolve first; `/generate` returns the resolved dict for the preview.
